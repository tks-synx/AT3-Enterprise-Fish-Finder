Nature of the Task
Task: You are to research and address a problem for a client that can be addressed using data. Define the problem clearly and determine the type of data required to address it before presenting your findings to the client.

You have the freedom to choose any issue. It could be anything from environmental concerns, social issues, economic trends, health data, or even something related to your hobbies and interests. The goal is to apply the skills you have learnt in Year 12 Enterprise Computing to solve a problem and present your findings in a clear and compelling way.

Sample Datasets

Throughout this project, you will:

Collect and analyse relevant data (from existing data sets and your own collection).
Create visualisations to represent your data and findings.
Use predictive analysis techniques to forecast future trends or outcomes.
Present your findings and learnings.
Example Breakdown
Scenario: Analysing and Predicting Traffic Patterns in a Major City

Introduction: In this project, you will analyse and predict traffic patterns in Sydney. Traffic congestion is a significant issue in urban areas, affecting daily commutes, economic productivity, and environmental sustainability. By leveraging data science, data visualisation, and predictive analysis, you will explore the factors contributing to traffic congestion and develop models to forecast future traffic trends.

Project Outline:

Project Planning and Data Collection

Define the scope of the project and identify key objectives.
Collect historical traffic data from sources such as city transportation departments, traffic APIs, or open data portals.
Gather additional data on weather conditions, public events, roadworks, and other factors that may influence traffic patterns.
Data Cleaning and Preprocessing

Clean and preprocess the collected data to ensure accuracy and consistency.
Handle missing values, outliers, and data normalisation.
Merge and integrate data from different sources.
Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA)

Perform exploratory data analysis to identify trends, patterns, and correlations in the data.
Learn the structure of the data
Uncover patterns or erros
Find relationships and insights
Crucial first step is good analysis
Use data visualisation techniques to create charts, graphs, and maps that illustrate traffic patterns.
Identify peak traffic hours, congestion hotspots, and factors influencing traffic flow.
Summary statistics
Measures of central tendency find the center of the dataset
Mean: The average value
Median: The middle value
Mode: The most common value
Range: Difference between the max and min
Feature Engineering

Create new features from the existing data that may improve the predictive models.
Consider factors such as time of day, day of the week, weather conditions, and special events.
Evaluate the importance of different features using statistical methods.
Model Development

Develop predictive models to forecast traffic patterns using machine learning algorithms.
Experiment with different models such as linear regression, decision trees, and neural networks.
Split the data into training and testing sets to evaluate model performance.
Model Evaluation and Optimisation

Evaluate the performance of the predictive models using metrics such as mean absolute error (MAE), root mean square error (RMSE), and R-squared.
Optimise the models by tuning hyperparameters and selecting the best-performing model.
Validate the models using cross-validation techniques.
Data Visualisation and Reporting

Create interactive dashboards and visualisations to present the findings.
Use tools such as KNIME, Tableau, Power BI, or Python libraries (e.g., Matplotlib, Seaborn) to create visual representations of traffic patterns and predictions.
Prepare a comprehensive report summarising the analysis, findings, and recommendations.
Scenario Analysis and What-If Simulations

Conduct scenario analysis to explore the impact of different factors on traffic patterns.
Perform what-if simulations to predict traffic under various conditions (e.g., road closures, public events, weather changes).
Analyse the potential effects of proposed traffic management strategies.
Presentation Preparation

Prepare a presentation to showcase the project findings and recommendations.
Create slides, visual aids, and talking points for the presentation.
Practice delivering the presentation to ensure clarity and confidence.
Final Presentation and Reflection

Present the project to peers, teachers, and stakeholders.
Receive feedback and answer questions from the audience.
Reflect on the project experience, challenges faced, and lessons learned.

Section 5b: Stand Up (15 marks)
‘The 'Stand Up' is a two-way discussion between examiner and student in which the student must demonstrate an understanding of the concepts in the assessment task. It is a technique where students engage in a dialogue, usually with questions and answers, and where a student can demonstrate their understanding of the subject and reveal the originality of their ideas.  

 

Students will be allocated random questions on the topics below:

Identifying/defining & Research/planning
Producing and implementing
Databases
Testing and evaluating
To prepare for the 'Stand Up':

You will only be asked questions created from the topics in the assessment task.
You may not bring any notes. 
Consider the following when completing your 'Stand Up':

Have you taken a moment to think about exactly what is being asked? It can be easy and tempting in stressful moments to just start talking. Think carefully and answer the question being asked.
Can you be more concise and clearer in your explanations? Practice becoming familiar with key terminology of the course and know how to use these terms. Keep in mind the allocated time frame so ensure to include your best examples.
How can you improve your answers and responses to questions? Practice and ask your peers for feedback on your responses. Go back and check all your information relating to the area and the concepts that apply to it.
Did you miss any opportunities to demonstrate an understanding of your area? You need to consider an effective way of communicating the concepts, using appropriate examples to support your discussion. Be positive, articulate and knowledgeable in the assessment. The markers are interested in finding out what you know about your topic.
Some types of questions that you may be asked:

Identifying/defining & Research/planning
What process did you undertake to determine the data, data types and structure of data to use in your project?
Producing and implementing
When conducting your risk analysis, what where the main issues that arose with your data?
Data Science/Visualisation/Intelligent Systems
What was the greatest barrier you faced in the creation of your system?
Testing and evaluating
How did you periodically modify your designs to improve functionality?
 

Students will be assessed on how well they:

respond to a set of impromptu questions
demonstrate an understanding of course content
use accurate and relevant examples to support your responses.
use evidence and examples from stimuli and their project to support responses
communicate information and ideas clearly and accurately using analysis, critical thinking and syllabus terminology
The 'Stand Up' will be 10-15 minutes long. You will need to take the initiative in demonstrating you have studied the area and have developed your own thoughts and analysis. It will be up to you to give the examiner enough information about the topic and to demonstrate depth of knowledge and focus of study. You will not be allowed to bring in any written material to the interview.

You need to remember that the 'Stand Up' will be timed and you should reveal your knowledge and understanding during that time. To start the 'Stand Up', a student may be asked a broad question, but it is vital that you get straight to the point and use examples as soon as possible. The examiners will then have the opportunity to ask you more specific questions about your observations. Be careful not to labour a point. You do not want to run out of time, and still have your best examples to come!

Questions are often broad but allow a student to give detailed and perceptive answers. A good working knowledge of the concepts is paramount.  The 'Stand Up' is a chance to demonstrate your knowledge and understanding of the chosen topic. With careful planning, consistent work and practice, it should be a positive and rewarding experience.

Milestone: Stand Ups will be conducted in Term 3 Week 1
